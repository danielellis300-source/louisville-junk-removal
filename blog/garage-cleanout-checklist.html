The Garage Cleanout Checklist: Where to Start

August 20, 2026 · 2 min read

A garage fills up because it's the path of least resistance — every item that doesn't have a home ends up there. Clearing it out isn't hard, but doing it in a random order turns a one-day job into a three-weekend one. Here's the sequence that works.

Step 1: Pick a dry day and empty it completely

Pull everything onto the driveway. All of it. You cannot make good keep-or-toss decisions about items buried three deep against a wall, and a half-sorted garage just becomes a differently-arranged full garage. A full empty-out takes a couple of hours and is the single most important step. Check the weather — you want a dry day with enough daylight to get it all back in or hauled by evening.

Step 2: Sort into five zones on the driveway

  • Keep — belongs in the garage: tools, lawn equipment, sports gear, seasonal items you actually use.
  • Keep — belongs elsewhere: stuff that drifted out here and needs to go back inside the house.
  • Donate/sell: working, usable items someone else wants.
  • Haul: broken, obsolete, water-damaged, or mystery items. Be honest here — this is usually the biggest pile.
  • Hazardous: paint, solvents, motor oil, gas cans, propane, pesticides, pool chemicals, old batteries.

Step 3: Deal with the hazardous pile properly

This pile does not go in the trash, the donate box, or the junk truck. Jefferson County residents take it to the Metro Government household hazardous waste drop-off; Floyd and Clark County residents use Southern Indiana's collection events. Latex paint can be hardened with kitty litter or a paint-hardener additive and thrown out once fully solid. Propane tanks go back to an exchange cage. Step 4: Put 'keep' back with a system

Before anything goes back in, decide on zones: wall-hung for long tools, shelving for bins, a high shelf for seasonal, floor space for mowers and bikes. Put things back by zone, not by whatever's closest. Clear plastic bins with labels beat cardboard boxes — you'll actually be able to find things, which is what keeps the garage from refilling.

Step 5: Handle the haul pile

For a typical two-car garage cleanout, the haul pile is usually a quarter to a half truckload — call it $200–$450 in volume-based pricing. If you're also doing a renovation and generating debris over several days, a dumpster might fit better. For a one-day clear-out where you just want it gone by dinner, book a crew for the afternoon: get everything into the haul zone in the morning, and the load-out takes about an hour.
