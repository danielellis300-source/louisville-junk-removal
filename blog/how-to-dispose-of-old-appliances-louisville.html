How to Dispose of Old Appliances in Louisville

August 15, 2026 · 2 min read

A dead refrigerator is one of the most awkward things to get rid of — too heavy to move alone, banned from the regular trash, and full of components that have to be handled a specific way. Here are the realistic options for offloading large appliances in Louisville and Southern Indiana, and what each one costs you in money and effort.

Why appliances can't just go to the curb

Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant that, by federal law, has to be professionally recovered before the unit is scrapped. Many also have components — compressors, capacitors, older units with mercury switches or PCB-containing parts — that make them regulated as scrap rather than trash. That's why Louisville's regular waste collection won't take them and why licensed haulers may charge a small recovery fee on those specific units.

Option 1: Retailer haul-away on delivery

If you're buying a replacement, almost every appliance retailer will haul the old unit away when they deliver the new one, often for free or a small fee. This is the least-effort path — but only works on a one-for-one swap, and the delivery crew won't take a second old appliance from the garage or disconnect anything that isn't the direct trade-in.

Option 2: Scrap and recycling yards

Metal recyclers in the Louisville area will take appliances, and some pay a small amount for the steel by weight. The catch is transport: you need a truck or trailer and at least one strong helper to load a washer or range, and units with refrigerant may be rejected or docked unless the refrigerant has already been recovered and tagged. Call ahead to confirm what a given yard accepts.

Option 3: LG&E and utility programs

Utility-run appliance recycling programs come and go, and terms change year to year — sometimes there's a rebate for turning in a working second fridge or freezer, sometimes there's a waitlist, sometimes the program is paused. If you have a working but energy-hungry spare appliance, it's worth checking whether a current program is running before you pay to have it hauled.

Option 4: Full-service pickup

A junk removal crew disconnects the appliance, carries it out from wherever it is — basement, second floor, back of the garage — loads it, and routes it to a recycler. Pricing follows the normal volume model: a single appliance is usually a minimum-load charge in the $120–$175 range, less per unit if it's part of a larger load. Units needing refrigerant recovery may add a small fee, disclosed when you book.
